Interacting shells in anti-de Sitter space revisited

Two-Shell AdS Orbits

Two concentric thin shells, A and B, move under their own gravity inside anti-de Sitter space, pass through each other, and trade energy at every crossing. Each head-on crossing adds a point to the Poincaré map. A twin, started 5×10−5 farther out, drifts slowly from a regular orbit and exponentially from a chaotic one.

More about the model

Two concentric thin shells, A and B, move under their own gravity inside anti-de Sitter space. Each shell is a fluid confined to a spherical surface, and its pressure resists contraction. In the orbits shown here,1 that pressure turns each contracting shell back out, and anti-de Sitter space turns each expanding shell back at a finite radius, before it reaches the boundary. The shells pass through each other and trade energy at every crossing. Each time they meet head on, with A moving out from the inside and B moving in from the outside, the crossing radius and A's energy just before the crossing are recorded as one point on the Poincaré map. A twin, started with both shells 5×10−5 farther out, shows how a nearby orbit drifts from this one. The log–log distance chart shows how the distance between them grows: roughly in proportion to the number of returns along the regular orbits here (at the fixed point it stays near its starting size), and exponentially along the two chaotic orbits, one in the layer around the saddle and one near the map's ends, until the twin can be anywhere the chaotic orbit goes. A finite run shows these patterns but cannot by itself prove chaos or regularity.

Not every start behaves this way. With other initial data the shells can collapse into a black hole instead, as the type (ii) data of Brito, Cardoso, and Rocha (BCR) do above a threshold mass. How far out a shell can go depends on w, the ratio of its pressure to its energy density. For w < 1/2, as for the shells here (w = 0.2), anti-de Sitter space turns every expanding shell back at a finite radius. For w > 1/2, a shell reaches the boundary in a finite time; BCR impose perfectly reflecting boundary conditions there, so it bounces back. Either way, no shell escapes.

This interactive demo is based on the paper “Interacting shells in anti-de Sitter space revisited: an invariant measure, regular orbits, and chaos” by Yusaku Sako.

About the data

Each orbit and its twin were computed with the code of the paper “Interacting shells in anti-de Sitter space revisited” (DOP853, relative tolerance 10−12, crossings located exactly). The two orbits from the paper's Fig. 3, in the chaotic layer and just outside it, follow the paper's own trajectories: their section points come from the same integrator and settings as Fig. 3 and match its stored points exactly, and the crossings within each return come from a one-return run of the solver. The orbit near the map's ends starts where the paper's Fig. 7 orbit does, at (R, 100 Mmid) = (0.19, 1.575), with the same settings; the edge of the map comes from the same calculation. The page stores the state after every crossing and integrates the smooth motion between crossings itself, restarting from the stored state at each crossing. Time is in units of the AdS radius.
